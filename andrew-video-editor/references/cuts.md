# Cut Language

## What makes a cut feel intentional

A good cut transfers attention. The viewer should arrive in the next shot already knowing where to look or why the change happened.

### Motivations

- **Action cut:** cut during movement; motion masks discontinuity.
- **Sound cut:** a transient, word, breath, impact, or musical accent motivates the picture change.
- **Gaze cut:** a look creates a question; the next shot answers it.
- **Reveal cut:** withhold information, then reveal at the moment of maximum usefulness.
- **Rhythmic cut:** duration pattern itself becomes expressive.
- **Contrast cut:** deliberately break composition, scale, brightness, or motion for impact.
- **Match cut:** preserve shape, movement, framing, or idea across the boundary.
- **Jump cut:** deliberately remove temporal continuity; use as a style, not by accident.

## Frame-level tuning

For a proposed impact at frame F, inspect F-2 through F+2. Compare:

- motion phase;
- subject pose;
- eye direction;
- transient location;
- brightest/strongest frame;
- whether the new shot lands too early or feels late.

Do not assume waveform peak equals perceptual cut point. Visual anticipation often makes a cut one frame before the peak feel stronger.

## Hard cut versus transition

Default to a hard cut. Add a transition only if it communicates time, place, state, memory, shock, or stylistic rhythm. A generic dissolve used merely because two shots do not connect is usually weaker than choosing better source frames.

## J and L cuts

- **J-cut:** next shot audio begins before its picture.
- **L-cut:** previous shot audio continues under the next picture.

Use them to make dialogue and ambience flow, but do not let music-sync logic accidentally truncate speech.
