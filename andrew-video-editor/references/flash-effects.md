# Fast Flash Effects

## The three useful families

### Full white / full black
A complete frame replacement. Highest perceived violence, useful at an impact or cut. Keep extremely short.

### Exposure bloom
The source remains visible but is blown out briefly. Better when continuity should remain readable through the flash.

### Flash-cut-flash
A flash straddles the boundary: last frame(s) of shot A and first frame(s) of shot B are partially or fully blown out. Useful for energetic music edits and for hiding small discontinuities.

## Duration guidance

At 24-30 fps, start with 2 frames. At 50-60 fps, start with 3 frames. Then inspect frame-by-frame.

Examples:

- 2 frames at 24 fps = 83.3 ms.
- 2 frames at 30 fps = 66.7 ms.
- 3 frames at 60 fps = 50 ms.

The same frame count does not mean the same duration at different frame rates.

## Placement

For a cut on frame F:

- flash ending at F makes the new shot feel like the consequence of the impact;
- flash starting at F makes the new shot feel like the source of the impact;
- flash centered on F can mask a small continuity jump.

Test all three when the moment matters.

## Restraint

A flash is punctuation. Repeated flashing quickly loses force and can create a photosensitivity hazard. Avoid sustained rapid sequences unless explicitly necessary and carefully controlled.
