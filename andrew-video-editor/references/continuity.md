# Continuity and the Pink-Elephant Rule

The “pink elephant” is any invariant the user has declared must remain unchanged across shots. It can be literal or symbolic: a person, prop, hand, tattoo, instrument, wardrobe detail, direction of travel, light source, object color, or story fact.

## Build a continuity lock list

For every continuity-sensitive sequence, record only what matters:

- identity / face / body;
- wardrobe and accessories;
- prop state and hand occupancy;
- subject position and screen direction;
- camera side of the action axis;
- lighting direction and time-of-day cues;
- background landmarks;
- motion phase entering/exiting the shot;
- any explicit user invariant.

## Boundary inspection

For each cut, compare at least:

- last two frames of outgoing shot;
- first two frames of incoming shot.

For high-motion cuts, inspect ±4 frames.

## Continuity triage

1. Choose a better frame range.
2. Choose a different take.
3. Reorder shots if story permits.
4. Use a cutaway/insert.
5. Use a very short flash/whip only for a small mismatch.
6. Regenerate footage only when editing cannot preserve the required invariant.

Do not use effects to disguise a major identity change.
